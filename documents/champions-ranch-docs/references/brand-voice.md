# Champions Ranch: Voice and Tone (for documents)

Write like a friend who discovered something real and is letting you in on it.
Warm, grounded, unhurried. We know what we do is good. We do not oversell. The
core promise is "Time worth spending."

## Voice attributes

| Attribute | Sounds like | Not like |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Warm | "We put together a plan we think will land well." | Cold, transactional |
| Grounded | "Horses, open fields, fire, farm-table meals." | Airy, abstract, luxury-speak |
| Unhurried | Short sentences. Space to breathe. | Rushed, pressure-driven |
| Authentic | "Not a resort. Something you have to feel." | Polished resort cliches |
| Locally rooted | "30 minutes from Sarjapur Road." | Globally generic |

## We are / we are not

We are a working experience center where something shifts. We are not a luxury
resort or a theme park. We are a friend letting you in on something good, not a
salesman. Rustic and warm, not corporate jargon. Specific to Bangalore and
Karnataka, not generic.

## Tone for corporate documents

Aim for roughly 70% formal, 30% warm. Lead with professionalism, back it with
warmth. Clean and itemised on the commercials, human in the framing. A proposal
opens with a short note before the numbers, lays out the experience plainly, and
closes with one honest, unhurried thought.

## Approved corporate headlines

- "The offsite they'll actually talk about."
- "No projectors. No icebreakers. Just horses, fire, and honest conversation."
- "Time worth spending."
- "Where Bangalore comes to breathe."
- "30 minutes from your desk. A world away."

## Words we love / avoid

Love: time worth spending, open sky, red earth, breathe, swap, honest
conversation, something you have to feel, working experience center, unplug,
reset, leave changed.

Avoid: luxury, resort, transform, holistic, synergy, best-in-class,
award-winning, limited-time, exclusive, AMAZING / INCREDIBLE / LIFE-CHANGING.
Replace "experience" on its own with "time worth spending" or "something you
have to feel."

## Mechanics

- No em-dashes, ever. Use periods, commas, colons, or restructure.
- Exclamation marks: at most one in an entire document, usually zero.
- Numbers: spell out under 10, numerals for 10+. Distances as "30 minutes from
  Sarjapur Road."
- Prices: rupee sign with Indian digit grouping. `₹1,500 per person`, `₹7,73,171`.
- Sentences average 10 to 15 words. Mix short and long. Fragments are fine.

## Reusable proposal copy

Opening (a note before the numbers):

> Thank you for considering Champions Ranch for your team's stay. We are glad you
> reached out, and we have put together a plan we think will land well with your
> group of [N]. Here is what we are not. We are not a luxury resort. We are not a
> theme park. Champions Ranch is a working experience center on the edge of
> Bangalore, 30 minutes from Sarjapur Road, where corporate teams come to step
> back, breathe, and spend time that actually matters. Horses, open fields,
> wellness, fire, farm-table meals, real conversation. No projectors. No forced
> icebreakers.

Closing thought:

> This is what an offsite looks like when no one is performing. Teams arrive in
> the evening, settle in around the fire, and by the next afternoon something has
> shifted. People talk differently. They listen longer. They leave changed.

Sign-off: "Warm regards, Champions Ranch Team." Footer lockup: "Champions Ranch
· An InfraTech Property · A Champions Group Experience · Time Worth Spending."
