# Deep Prep Template — SPIN-Driven Client Dossier

> This template produces meeting preps in Sreedeep's signature style: mildly irreverent,
> consultative, client-first, and pain-point driven. Every section earns its place by
> connecting back to the client's reality through the SPIN framework.
>
> If a section sounds like it could've been written for any company, rewrite it.
> If it sounds like a vendor pitch, set it on fire and start over.

## 0. Continuity Section *(follow-up meetings only)*

When this is NOT the first meeting, add this section at the top. It's the difference
between "nice to meet you again" and "here's exactly where we left off."

```markdown
## Previous Meeting Continuity

**Last Meeting:** [Date] — [1 sentence: what was the vibe? productive? exploratory? tense?]

### What We Committed To
| # | Commitment | Owner | Status | Evidence/Notes |
|---|-----------|-------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | ... | Us / Them | Done/In Progress/Overdue | ... |

### What They Pushed Back On
[Don't sugarcoat it. If they had concerns about pricing, data accuracy, or "do we really
need this?", acknowledge it here and prepare a response.]

### What's Changed Since
- [Their side] New developments, funding, personnel changes, market shifts
- [Our side] New capabilities, case studies, wins relevant to their situation
- [Market] Industry changes that affect the SPIN narrative
```

## 1. Meeting Goal & Agenda

### Primary Objective
One clear sentence framed as an outcome, not a task:
- "Secure agreement for a $25K-$50K pilot targeting U.S. radiology decision-makers"
- NOT: "Present LakeB2B capabilities" (what are you, a brochure?)

### Desired Outcomes
3-4 specific, checkable-after-the-meeting outcomes.

### Stepwise Agenda (SPIN-Sequenced)

The agenda follows the SPIN arc. Early phases build understanding (Situation, Problem),
middle phases create urgency (Implication), and later phases unlock value (Need-Payoff):

| Time | Phase | SPIN Stage | Activity |
|------|-------|------------|----------|
| 0-5 min | Opening | — | Relationship context, rapport, "what's new since last time" |
| 5-15 min | Discovery | S+P | Client shares their current state and what's not working |
| 15-25 min | Deepening | P+I | Explore the cost/risk of those problems continuing |
| 25-35 min | Value Framing | N | "Here's what becomes possible..." — solutions as outcomes |
| 35-45 min | Specifics | N | Pilot structure, timeline, scope, pricing |
| 45-50 min | Next Steps | — | Action items with owners and dates |

## 3. SPIN Question Sequences

This replaces generic "discovery questions." Each question sequence is tailored to
a specific attendee and follows the SPIN progression deliberately.

### SPIN Sequence for [CEO/Founder Name]

**Situation Questions** (establish baseline — show you've done homework, don't ask what
you can Google):
1. "Post-[milestone], what does your [market] playbook look like today — [option A],
   [option B], or [option C]?" [*Already know the answer partially — this validates*]
2. "Of your [X] installations, what's the split between [segment A] and [segment B]?"
   [*Maps to ICP data delivery*]

**Problem Questions** (surface pain — let THEM say it, don't tell them):
3. "Where do deals stall most — awareness, evaluation, procurement, or integration?"
   [*Identifies pipeline bottleneck → maps to specific solution*]
4. "How does your team currently identify and reach decision-makers at scale?"
   [*If the answer is "manually" or "it's hard" → that's the pain*]

**Implication Questions** (make the pain expensive — what happens if they DON'T fix it):
5. "With [competitor] at [X countries/installations], what happens to your market
   position if the [specific market] entry takes 12 months instead of 3?"
   [*Creates urgency without being alarmist*]
6. "What's the cost of a 6-month delay in building pipeline infrastructure —
   in terms of market share, not just revenue?"
   [*Forces them to quantify inaction*]

**Need-Payoff Questions** (let THEM articulate the value — don't pitch, ask):
7. "What if you could go from [current state] to [5,000 qualified leads] in
   [4 weeks] — without hiring a single person?"
   [*Uses real numbers from research*]
8. "Would a joint [specific initiative] move the needle for your [specific goal]?"
   [*Lets them sell the solution internally*]

### SPIN Sequence for [Director/VP Name]
[Same structure, different lens — operational, not strategic]

### Pitch-Driving Questions (conversation closers)
2-3 questions that use concrete data to create "aha moments":
- "What if we could deliver [specific, researched deliverable] within [realistic timeline]?"
- These should feel inevitable, not hypothetical.

## 4. Situation Analysis (Company Research)

### [Company Name] — The Reality Check

**Company Overview:** 2-3 paragraphs that read like a briefing, not a Wikipedia entry.
Include what's going well, what's at a crossroads, and what's missing. The tone should
be "trusted advisor reviewing the board deck," not "intern summarizing the About page."

**Key Metrics:**

| Metric | Data | So What? |
|--------|------|----------|
| Founded | Year, Location | [What stage are they at?] |
| Employees | Count + growth | [Resource constraints?] |
| Revenue | ARR/revenue | [How does this compare to competitors?] |
| Funding | Rounds, investors | [Runway? Growth pressure?] |
| Key Milestones | Regulatory, product | [What doors did this open?] |

The "So What?" column is what separates a research dump from a SPIN situation analysis.
Every metric should connect to a potential Problem or Implication.

**Product/Service Breakdown:** Table with Product, Function, Impact.

**Business Model:** How they make money, pricing, what works and what might not scale.

## 5. Problem & Implication Analysis (Pain Points)

This is the CORE of the prep. Not a generic "challenges" section — a prosecutorial
case for why the status quo is unacceptable.

### Pain Points & Implication Matrix

| Pain Point | Evidence (sourced) | Implication (cost of inaction) | SPIN Question That Surfaces It |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| [Specific pain] | [From research, not assumption] | [Time/money/competitive cost] | [Which question in Section 3?] |

**Rules for this table:**
- Every pain point needs EVIDENCE. "They probably need better marketing" = garbage.
  "Their website doesn't rank for a single high-intent keyword in a market where
  competitors have 10K+ monthly searches" = evidence.
- The Implication column should make someone wince. Not "they might miss opportunities"
  but "every month without U.S. pipeline infrastructure, the 12-month window between
  FDA clearance and Qure.ai's expansion narrows."
- The SPIN Question column ensures every pain is surfaced through dialogue, not assertion.
  You don't TELL them their marketing is broken. You ASK them how their team currently
  generates pipeline, and let the answer reveal the gap.

### Role-Based Pain Framing

| Attendee | Their #1 Pain | Why They Care | How to Surface It |
|----------|--------------|---------------|-------------------|
| CEO | Market window | Legacy, board pressure | Implication question about competitive timing |
| VP/Director | Resource gap | Can't deliver on strategy | Problem question about team capacity |
| Technical | Integration risk | Their reputation | Situation question about current stack |

## 6. Need-Payoff Framing & Solution Mapping

Every solution is framed as what the client GAINS, not what we SELL.

### Solution Mapping (Pain → Capability → Outcome)

| Pain Point | Our Capability | Client Outcome | Proof Point |
|------------|---------------|----------------|-------------|
| [From Section 5] | [Specific service/tool] | [What becomes possible] | [Case study, data point, or demo] |

**The reframing principle:**
- "We offer email marketing services" = feature dump
- "Go from zero U.S. pipeline to 5,000 qualified radiology leads in 4 weeks —
  without hiring a single marketing person" = need-payoff

Every row should pass the "so what" test from the client's perspective.

Frame it as context that makes the client's situation more urgent or more promising.

### Competitive Landscape

|-----------|-----|--------------|----------------------|-----------|
| ... | ... | ... | Where client wins | How we amplify this advantage |

The "Our Angle" column connects the competitive analysis back to SPIN Need-Payoff.

### Client's Competitive Moat
1-2 paragraphs that respect the client's position. This isn't flattery — it's strategic
validation that shows you understand what makes them worth partnering with. If you can't
articulate why they're special, you haven't researched hard enough.

| **External** | Opportunities: ... | Threats: ... |

**LakeB2B/Ampliz/Champions SWOT (in context of THIS partnership):**

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|----------|----------|
| **Internal** | Strengths: ... | Weaknesses: ... |
| **External** | Opportunities: ... | Threats: ... |

Including our weaknesses isn't self-sabotage — it's preparation. If the client asks
"but you're not a healthcare company, are you?" Sreedeep already has the response ready.

### The Management Narrative

This is the section that gets forwarded to the client's board. Write it accordingly.

**Structure:**
1. **Acknowledge** where the client is today — with specific data, not puffery
2. **Quantify** the cost of the status quo — make inaction feel expensive
3. **Position** the partnership as strategic infrastructure, not a vendor relationship
4. **Close** with the "with vs. without" comparison table

The tone should be direct, confident, and slightly provocative. Not arrogant, but not
meek either. Think "the consultant who tells the CEO what their team won't."

**Competitive USP Table:**

| Factor | Without LakeB2B | With LakeB2B |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| [Factor tied to pain point] | [Status quo cost] | [Need-payoff outcome] |

Every row in this table should trace back to a pain point from Section 5.
If it doesn't connect to a client pain, it doesn't belong here.
