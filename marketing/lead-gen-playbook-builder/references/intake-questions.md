# Intake Questions: From a one-line idea to a specified campaign

Use this to flesh out a rough campaign idea. Extract answers from the conversation, the vault, connected data, and research first. Only ask the user the gaps that genuinely block the build, and batch them into one question. Convert relative dates to absolute. The goal is to lock four things: WHO, WHAT, WHY NOW, and the CONSTRAINTS.

## A. The offer (WHAT)

1. What are we actually selling or inviting people to? Name the product, service, event, or asset in one sentence.
2. What is the single most compelling outcome for the buyer? Not the feature, the result.
3. What proof do we have? Named customers, case studies, confirmed attendees, data, numbers. Flag which proof is genuinely confirmed vs aspirational. (This gates anchor discipline.)
4. What is the price, and what is the pricing posture? Free, indicative range, firm quote, gated on scope. Who must sign off on a firm number?

## B. The audience (WHO)

5. Who is the ideal target? Titles, seniority, industries, company size, geography.
6. Is there a real contact dataset? If yes, profile it: title distribution, industry distribution, company-size distribution, data quality. Build personas from the actual distribution, not assumptions.
7. How warm is the list? Cold, opted-in, prior relationship, event attendees. This sets the cadence aggressiveness and compliance posture.
8. Who are the anchor accounts or names that pull the rest? (Confirmed only.)

## C. The trigger (WHY NOW)

9. Why should the prospect care this week, not next quarter? A moment, a deadline, a regulation, a competitive pressure, a seasonal spike.
10. What is the prospect's internal conversation right now that we can name back to them? (The best openers name the prospect's own mental model.)

## D. The constraints

11. What is the timeline? Hard date, window, or always-on. Work backward from it.
12. Which channels are available and resourced? Email infra, LinkedIn (Sales Nav?), WhatsApp, phone, social, newsletter, paid.
13. What is the rep capacity? How many reps, how many hours, what daily volume is realistic.
14. What is the win condition? The specific number that defines success (qualified conversations, samples shipped, RSVPs, closed deals, GMV) by a specific date.
15. What approvals or decisions block execution, and who owns each? (Decision-blocked channels that are built but never activated are a classic failure.)
16. What compliance applies? Consumer opt-in (CAN-SPAM, TCPA), regulated verticals (betting, finance, health), trademark constraints, minors (never target).

## E. The failure modes to pre-empt

17. What killed the last similar campaign, if there was one? Bake the fix in.
18. Where could a cascade happen? (Anchor dropout, deliverability collapse, sample-SLA miss.)

## Output of intake

A short locked brief: offer, proof (confirmed vs aspirational), 3-6 persona seeds, the why-now, channels, capacity, win condition by date, approvals map, compliance posture, and the named failure modes to design against. Everything downstream builds on this.
