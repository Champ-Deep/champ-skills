# Channel Playbooks: voice, structure, cadence, and pitch templates

Each channel has a job. Matching the message to the channel's job is what makes a multi-touch sequence feel like a person who respects the prospect, not a machine spraying the same line six ways. This file gives the per-channel voice, the cadence patterns, and reusable pitch templates. In a real playbook, write the actual copy per persona; these are the scaffolds.

## The pitch-round model (applies across all channels)

Every touch belongs to a round. The round, not the channel, decides how hard you push.

| Round | Job | Does | Never does | CTA |
|-------|-----|------|-----------|-----|
| R1 Interest | Earn attention | Names the prospect's problem or moment | Lists features, quotes price, names the SKU | Low-commitment chat OR free value |
| R2 Sample/Value | Prove value | Offers something useful with no strings | Asks for a meeting before value is seen | Sample, resource, or value-first ask |
| R3 Offer | Close | The actual offer, options, indicative price | Re-opens with the asset if R1/R2 got no engagement | Decision OR clean file close |

The litmus: if your first line could be sent to a competitor's prospect and still make sense, it is an R3 line in an R1 slot. Rewrite it.

## The channel waterfall (per prospect, not per list)

You are not narrowing a list across channels. You are escalating commitment with the same prospect across surfaces. A typical tiered waterfall:

### Tier 1 (named, high-value, rep-owned)
| # | Day | Channel | Round | What goes out |
|---|-----|---------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | 0 | Email | R1 | Problem/moment opener, two CTAs of unequal commitment |
| 2 | 2 | Social DM | R1 | Contextual reply to their content, soft mention of the email |
| 3 | 4 | LinkedIn connect + note | R1 | Connection note only, no pitch |
| 4 | 6 | LinkedIn DM | R2 | Sample/value hook |
| 5 | 8 | Phone | R2 | Warm call + voicemail script ready |
| 6 | 10 | Email | R3 | The offer, options, indicative price |
| 7 | 12 | Email | R3 | Breakup, file close with a future re-open |

### Tier 2 (sequenced, lighter)
| # | Day | Channel | Round |
|---|-----|---------|-------|
| 1 | 0 | Email | R1 |
| 2 | 5 | LinkedIn connect | R1 |
| 3 | 8 | Email | R2 |
| 4 | 14 | Email | R3 + breakup |

### Tier 3 (nurture only)
Bulk newsletter slot with the moment hook + value CTA, then one R3 re-evaluation later.

Why a waterfall and not a blast: single-channel cold email replies run ~2-5% for B2B in current conditions; email + LinkedIn + phone combined runs ~12-18%. The waterfall is the difference between hitting and missing the target.

## Per-channel voice and templates

### Email
The workhorse. R1 names the problem; the body is short, two CTAs of unequal commitment so the lazy reply still converts. Never quote price in R1/R2. Rotate subject lines (max ~25% of a list on one subject). Verified addresses only, never info@/hello@, never re-mail bounces, cap sends per inbox per day.

```
Subject: {{prospect's own internal question}}

{{first_name}}, [name the conversation they're already having].
[One sentence that shows you understand their world.]
[Hint at value without claiming expertise.]

Worth {{low-commitment ask}} or want me to send {{free value}} first?

{{rep_first}}
{{brand}} | {{tagline}}
```

### LinkedIn (connect note + DM are different surfaces)
The connection note is NOT a pitch. It is an invitation to remember the email. The pitch goes in the DM a few days later.

```
Connect note: {{first_name}}, sent you a note on {{angle}} for {{their world}}.
Connecting because your {{roster/team/space}} will be in this conversation
regardless of whether we work together. Open to a quick chat if useful.

DM (R2): {{first_name}}, follow-up. Easiest way to see if this is useful is
to look at the actual {{sample/value}}. Want me to send {{specific thing}}?
No strings.
```

### WhatsApp (warm/known contacts only)
Highest response rate by far in relationship-led markets (often many times email), but only for contacts who expect to hear from you. Short, human, no formatting. Never cold-WhatsApp a stranger.

```
Hi {{first_name}}, {{rep_first}} here from {{brand}}. {{one warm, specific line}}.
Worth a quick look? Happy to send {{value}} or grab 15 mins.
```

### Phone (Tier 1 only)
Carries urgency the inbox cannot. Always have the voicemail script ready; most calls hit voicemail and the voicemail is the touch.

```
Call: {{first_name}}, {{rep_first}} from {{brand}}. Caught you between meetings
probably, won't keep you. {{the why-now in one line}}. Sending {{value}} regardless.
Email subject is "{{subject}}". Worth 15 mins?

Voicemail: Hi {{first_name}}, {{rep_first}} from {{brand}}. Calling about {{why-now}}.
Sending {{value}} by end of day regardless. Email subject: "{{subject}}". Thanks.
```

### Social DM (X / Threads / Instagram)
Only for prospects who post publicly. The whole value is that the message is contextual to their content. If they don't post, skip this touch.

```
Saw your take on {{topic}}. Curious how you're thinking about {{adjacent angle}}.
{{one specific, non-pitchy observation}}.
```

### Newsletter / bulk (Tier 3 nurture)
The moment hook plus a single value CTA to the whole nurture base. Low effort, wide net, sets up a later R3.

## Cadence discipline
- Each contact should get touched 3-7 times across the window depending on tier.
- Daily quotas per rep keep volume honest (e.g., 50 emails, 10 social DMs, 20 LinkedIn connects, 8 dials, 5 sample fulfilments).
- Stop the sequence the moment a prospect engages; switch to the reply-handling flow.
