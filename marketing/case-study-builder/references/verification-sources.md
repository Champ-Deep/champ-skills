# Verification Sources Guide

A claim in a case study that turns out to be wrong — or that the client disputes — is worse
than no case study at all. It damages the brand, strains the client relationship, and hands
ammunition to competitors. Verify before you write.

This file covers how to validate each type of evidence and what to do when something cannot
be confirmed.

---

## Primary Verification Sources

### 1. CS and File Delivery Records (Lake B2B)

**Who:** Sean (Customer Service and File Delivery, Lake B2B)
**What he can confirm:**
- Exactly what was delivered: contact count, segment composition (job titles, geographies, industries)
- When it was delivered and to whom (which Lake B2B AM sent it, which contact at the client received it)
- Any post-delivery exchanges (bounce reports, replacement requests, re-sends)
- Data quality confirmation from the client side if it was captured

**How to request:** Ask the user to check with Sean directly, or flag: "Sean can pull the delivery
record for [client name] [approximate date]. Can you ask him to confirm the contact count and any
client feedback on record?"

**When critical:** Any claim involving data volume, data quality, or deliverable specifications
must run through Sean before publishing.

### 2. Campaign Reports and Delivery Files

**What these confirm:**
- Email open rates, click rates, response rates from outreach campaigns
- Contact list volumes (records delivered, how many were used)
- Campaign send dates and timelines

**Where to find:**
- The outreach platform used (Champmail, campaign tool, or the client's own system)
- The account manager for the relevant company
- Sean for the underlying data/list component

**Framing note:** If the open rate comes from the client's own tool (their ESP, not yours),
it is client self-reported data. Frame it as: "According to [client]'s campaign tracking,
the list generated a 32% open rate..." rather than presenting it as your own metric.

### 3. Direct Client Communication on Record

**What this confirms:** Client satisfaction, approval of being named, specific quotes
**Where to find:** WhatsApp messages, email threads, account manager call notes
**How to use:** A WhatsApp message from Varun at VertexGrid saying "solid, we can move ahead"
after reviewing the data sample is real evidence of quality approval. It can be paraphrased
in the case study as a proof point even if not directly quotable.

**Approval requirement:** Before naming a client publicly, you need explicit approval —
either via email ("You can use our name/logo in your marketing") or through the AM who
has the relationship. A verbal "yes" relayed through the AM is workable if documented.

### 4. CRM and Internal Records

**What these confirm:** Contract scope, deal value, renewal history, referrals
**Where to find:** The relevant company's CRM, the account manager, or the finance team

## Verification Risk Levels

| Risk Level | Situation | Action |
|------------|-----------|--------|
| Low | All metrics from your own records, client approval in writing | Proceed |
| Medium | Metrics are client self-reported; approval is verbal via AM | Proceed with framing caveats. Note source in copy. |
| High | Metrics unconfirmed, client approval unclear, or data only from memory | Stop. Get written confirmation before proceeding. |
| Critical | Client has not been told they are being featured, or dispute risk is high | Do not proceed. Resolve approval first. |

---

## When Verification Fails

If a Tier 1 evidence item cannot be verified, you have three options:

**Option 1: Pause and get the data.** This is always the right answer for hard metrics.
Prompt the user with a specific, actionable ask: "Can you ask Sean to pull the April 14
delivery report for VertexGrid? We need the contact count and the segment breakdown."

**Option 2: Downgrade to Tier 2 framing.** If the hard number cannot be confirmed but the
qualitative outcome can, reframe: instead of "delivered 378 CXO contacts," write "a curated
list of Bangalore-based CXO contacts across the technology infrastructure sector."

**Option 3: Anonymize or generalize.** When the specific figure creates risk but the outcome
category is solid, generalize: "hundreds of verified CXO contacts" instead of "378."

---

## Self-Reported vs. Verified Data: Framing Guide

| Data Type | Attribution Language |
|-----------|---------------------|
| From your own campaign/delivery records | "[Metric] from [Company]'s delivery records" or simply state it as fact |
| Client self-reported (from their platform) | "According to [Client]'s tracking..." or "[Client] reported..." |
| Verbal, via AM, not in writing | "Following the engagement, [Client] confirmed..." (paraphrase only) |
| Inferred/estimated | Do not use as a hard metric. Reference qualitative outcome instead. |

## Quick Reference: Who Verifies What (Lake B2B Context)

| Claim type | Who verifies |
|------------|-------------|
| Data volume (contacts delivered) | Sean (CS/delivery) |
| Data quality (client approved samples) | Sean + the AM + client communication on record |
| Campaign open/response rates | AM + the outreach platform |
| Client approval to name/quote | AM who holds the relationship |
| Deal scope and contract value | Finance team or CRM |
| Follow-on business / renewal | AM or account records |
