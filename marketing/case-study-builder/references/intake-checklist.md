# Evidence Intake Checklist

This checklist defines what you need to collect before writing. Evidence is tiered by criticality.
A case study with only Tier 1 evidence is publishable but thin. Tier 2 makes it compelling. Tier 3
makes it exceptional.

---

## Tier 1: Must-Have (Cannot write without these)

These are non-negotiable. If any of these are missing, do not start writing. Go back to CS or the
user to get them first.

| # | Evidence Item | What to Ask / Where to Get It |
|---|---------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Client identity** | Name and company (for named case study) OR industry + company size (for anonymous). Anonymous case studies convert at roughly 60% of the rate of named ones. Push for named when possible. |
| 2 | **Client's challenge** | What was their situation BEFORE they came to you? What was it costing them — time, revenue, missed opportunities? This is the emotional anchor of the whole piece. |
| 3 | **What they purchased** | Exactly what product or service. For data: list type, size, ICP spec (industry, title, geography). For campaigns: channel, volume, cadence. |
| 4 | **At least one hard outcome metric** | One real number. Leads generated, response rate, conversion rate, pipeline value, deals closed, ROI. If CS cannot provide a number, the case study cannot be published as a proof document. |
| 5 | **The "after" state** | What is different for them now? Not just the metric — what did the result enable them to do? Did they hire? Expand? Hit a revenue milestone? The cascade matters. |

---

## Tier 2: Strong to Have (Significantly improves impact)

These are not blockers but they transform a thin case study into a convincing one.

| # | Evidence Item | Notes |
|---|---------------|-------|
| 6 | **Timeline** | How long from purchase to first result? How long to full outcome? Prospects want to know how fast they can expect this to work. "47 leads in 6 weeks" is dramatically more compelling than "47 leads." |
| 7 | **Specific campaign or list details** | What industry was the data for? What job titles? What was the email subject line that got the most opens? These details help the reader imagine themselves in the story. |
| 8 | **Before vs. after comparison** | What were they using or doing before (if anything)? How does that compare to what we delivered? Even "previously generating 3 leads per month, now generating 18" is powerful. |
| 9 | **Client quote or testimonial** | Even one sentence from the client in their own voice. The most trusted voice in a case study is never yours — it's the client's. If no formal testimonial exists, ask CS if there was anything said on a call that could be paraphrased or confirmed. |
| 10 | **Second outcome metric** | A second data point validates the first and suggests a pattern. E.g., "Response rate of 14% (3x industry average) and 31 booked meetings." |

---

## Tier 3: Nice to Have (Makes it exceptional)

| # | Evidence Item | Notes |
|---|---------------|-------|
| 11 | **Campaign creative examples** | Subject lines, email snippets, outreach sequences. Gives the reader something tangible to react to. |
| 12 | **Data / report screenshots** | Dashboard exports, campaign analytics, reply screenshots (anonymized if needed). Visual proof is the most credible kind. |
| 13 | **Client context** | How big is the company? How long have they been a client? Growth in spend over time? Increased purchase ticket size is itself a proof point — clients who get results come back and buy more. |
| 14 | **Future engagement** | Are they expanding? Renewing? Buying a different product? A client who renewed is a client who believed in the results. |
| 15 | **Permission level** | Can we use their logo? Their name? A photo? Formal permission (even a Slack message) matters for legal and for design. |

---

## Completeness Threshold Guide

| Evidence level | Assessment | Recommendation |
|----------------|------------|----------------|
| All Tier 1 present | Publishable | Proceed to writing. Note what is missing from Tier 2 for future enrichment. |
| Tier 1 present, Tier 2 partially | Strong case study | Proceed. Flag the missing Tier 2 items as follow-up for CS or client. |
| Tier 1 fully present + Tier 2 mostly present | Exceptional case study | Proceed. This will move the needle. |
| Tier 1 items missing | Not ready | Stop. List exactly which items are missing and who can get them (CS, client, campaign records). |

---

## Where CS Can Help

When going back to CS for data, be specific about what you need. Vague requests get vague answers.

Good ask: "For [Client], can you pull: (1) total leads delivered, (2) the date range of the campaign,
(3) the industry and title targeting, and (4) any positive feedback they gave on calls or over email?"

Bad ask: "Can you get me data on how [Client] is doing?"

CS should also be the source for identifying clients with the highest increase in purchase ticket
size — those clients are implicitly telling you the results are working. Sort by delta between
first purchase and most recent purchase. The clients at the top of that list are your best case
study candidates.
