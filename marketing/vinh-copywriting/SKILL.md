# The Vinh Giang Writing Method

> "Simple words work. Complicated ones don't."
> — Vinh Giang

This skill translates Vinh Giang's spoken communication and storytelling mastery into a repeatable system for written copy. Vinh is an award-winning keynote speaker, communication coach, and magician who teaches that communication — like magic — is a simple process practiced until it feels effortless. His core insight: **the moment your audience stops understanding you, you've lost them.**

This is a **writing layer** — a companion methodology that other skills invoke when they need sharper, more human, more persuasive text. It is not a standalone content generator. It is the quality filter that makes emails land, slides stick, pitches convert, and talking points feel natural.

## The 6 Laws of Vinh-Method Copy

These six laws govern every piece of writing produced under this skill. They are non-negotiable. Internalize them before writing a single word.

### Law 1: The Complexity Ladder — Always Descend

Any idea can be expressed at 5 levels of complexity. Most business writing sits at Level 3-5. Effective copy lives at Level 1-2.

| Level | What It Sounds Like | Example |
|-------|---------------------|---------|
| 1 | Conversational, a friend explaining over coffee | "We find the right people for your sales team to call." |
| 2 | Clear professional, zero jargon | "We provide verified contact data so your outreach lands." |
| 3 | Light jargon, still followable | "We deliver intent-qualified B2B contact intelligence for targeted outreach." |
| 4 | Dense, insider language | "Our multi-signal intent enrichment pipeline surfaces in-market decision-makers across your TAM." |
| 5 | Incomprehensible to outsiders | "Leveraging proprietary technographic + firmographic + psychographic data fusion, we enable omnichannel ABM orchestration across the full revenue waterfall." |

**The rule:** Write at Level 1-2. If a sentence creeps to Level 3+, rewrite it. Read it out loud — if you'd never say it to a friend, delete the jargon.

**Self-test:** At which level does your reader stop understanding you? That's where you've lost the deal, the click, the reply. Back up one level.

### Law 2: Your Copy Has a Voice — Earn It

Most business copy defaults to a habitual corporate drone: passive voice, hedge words, empty superlatives. That's not the brand's real voice — it's just a habit.

**Earning your written voice means:**
- Stripping away reflexive corporate language ("leverage," "synergize," "cutting-edge")
- Finding the words the founder/CEO would actually use in conversation
- Making the copy sound like a specific human, not a committee

**Before (habitual voice):**
> "Our industry-leading platform leverages cutting-edge AI to deliver best-in-class solutions that drive transformative business outcomes."

**After (earned voice):**
> "Our AI tool tells your reps which leads to call first."

### Law 3: Vary the Speed — Rhythm Is Meaning

In writing, rhythm = sentence length variation.

- **Short sentences punch.** They carry weight. They land.
- Longer sentences carry the reader through a thought, building momentum, stacking ideas, creating a flow that pulls them forward before they even realize they've read three lines.
- Then you stop.

**The pattern:** Long → short. Build → punch. Explain → land.

Short sentences land a specific fact, not a slogan. Stacked fragments ("They land. Every time.") read as AI output; keep one short sentence per key point at most.

Monotone sentence length is the written equivalent of speaking in a flat drone. Mix it up. Let the important stuff breathe in its own short sentence.

### Law 4: The Pause — Whitespace Is Persuasion

In copy, **the pause is whitespace.**

- One idea per paragraph.
- Let a powerful line stand alone.
- Don't cram three thoughts into one block of text.

Instead:

Let a powerful line stand alone.

Give the reader time to absorb before you move on. Strategic whitespace signals confidence. Dense text signals anxiety.

### Law 5: P.A.R.A. — The Anti-Ramble Structure

Vinh's P.A.R.A. framework prevents rambling. It works even better in copy:

| Step | In Speech | In Copy |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **P**oint | State your belief clearly | Lead with the headline / hook / value prop |
| **A**ction | What you did about it | Show what the product/service does (specifics, not platitudes) |
| **R**esult | What happened | Proof — numbers, outcomes, testimonials |
| **A**sk | Throw the ball back | CTA — tell the reader exactly what to do next |

Every email, every slide, every pitch paragraph should follow P.A.R.A. If you can't identify all four elements, something is missing.

### Law 6: The Storytelling Engine — Make Them Feel It

From Vinh's storytelling framework: stories make facts stick to the brain. A pitch without a story is forgettable. An email with a story gets replied to. Three ingredients turn flat copy into something people remember:

**Ingredient 1: Be Specific (5 Senses + Emotion)**

Most copy is too general. "We helped a client grow" means nothing. Specificity activates mirror neurons — the reader's brain simulates the experience.

- **Sight:** "The three interviewers were wearing dark suits, staring at me like I was on trial."
- **Sound:** "The only thing I could hear was the clicking of a pen."
- **Touch:** "My palms were so sweaty the paper in my hands began to wrinkle."
- **Emotion (the gamechanger):** Don't just describe what happened — describe the wave of panic, the relief, the moment of "I don't belong here."

**In copy:** Replace "We helped Company X improve results" with "Company X's sales team was calling 200 people a day and booking 3 meetings. After 60 days with us, same 200 calls, 14 meetings."

**Ingredient 2: Relive, Don't Report**

Reporting is flat. It's the Wikipedia version. Reliving is transformation.

- **Reporting:** "Last quarter we signed a new client. They were happy with the results."
- **Reliving:** "The email landed at 9:47 AM. By noon, their VP of Sales had forwarded it to three people. By Friday, we had a signed contract."

How to switch: Use present tense. Change energy with the moment. Let the reader feel the timeline.

**Ingredient 3: Share the Meaning**

A story without meaning is a campfire tale. In business copy, every story must bridge to the reader's world with the magic phrase: **"The reason I'm telling you this is because..."**

The audience doesn't want to know what happened to you. They want to know what it means for *them*. Story = doorway. Meaning = the room you're leading them into.

**When to use stories in copy:**
- Email body (especially cold outreach) → One micro-story, 2-3 sentences max
- Pitch decks / talking points → One anchor story per major section
- Follow-up emails → "Here's what happened with a similar company..."
- Slide notes → Story as the speaker's verbal bridge, not on the slide itself
- Proposals → Case study as narrative, not as bullet points

**When NOT to use stories:**
- Subject lines → Be direct
- CTA buttons → Be clear
- Data tables → Let numbers speak

## Application Playbooks

### Email Copy (Cold Outreach, Nurture, Follow-ups)

**Framework: 3-Line Cold Email**

Apply P.A.R.A. compressed to its tightest form:

```
Subject: [Point — the one thing they care about]

Hey [Name],

[1 sentence: Point — what you believe about their problem]
[1-2 sentences: Action + Result — what you did for someone like them, with a number]
[1 sentence: Ask — specific, low-friction next step]

[Signature]
```

**Example — Before (Level 4):**
> Subject: Exploring Strategic Partnership Opportunities in Data-Driven Customer Acquisition
>
> Dear [Name], I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out to introduce our company, which specializes in providing comprehensive B2B data intelligence solutions...

**Example — After (Level 1-2 Vinh method):**
> Subject: Your sales team is calling the wrong people
>
> Hey Sarah,
>
> 68% of outbound calls go to people who'll never buy. We fixed that for Acme Corp, and their connect rate jumped 3x in 60 days.
>
> Worth a 15-min call to see if we can do the same for [Company]?

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Subject line is Level 1 (a friend could say it)
- [ ] No sentence over 20 words
- [ ] Exactly one CTA
- [ ] A real number or proof point
- [ ] Contains a micro-story or specific outcome (Law 6)
- [ ] Would you actually say this out loud? If not, rewrite.

### Talking Points / Speaking Notes

**Framework: Vinh's Voice-to-Page Method**

Since this method is rooted in speech, talking points should feel like natural speech captured on paper.

1. Open with a question or bold statement (creates the "lean-in" moment)
2. Use the P.A.R.A. structure for each point
3. Write in short spoken beats where a speaker would pause: "Most teams blame their reps. / The reps are fine. / They are calling a list that went stale in March." Never open with "Here's the thing" and never use the "Not because X. Because Y." shape (see `no-ai-slop`).
4. End every section with a transition question or bridge phrase
5. Mark **[PAUSE]** where the speaker should let a point breathe
6. Include one anchor story per major talking point (Law 6 — Relive, don't Report)

### Slide Copy (for pptx / power-design)

**Framework: The 6-Word Slide Rule**

Slides are not documents. They are visual cues for a speaker. Apply these rules:

1. **Headlines only.** No slide title should exceed 8 words. Aim for 6.
2. **One idea per slide.** If you have two thoughts, you have two slides.
3. **Complexity Level 1.** If the audience can't grasp the slide in 3 seconds, simplify.
4. **Speaker notes carry the story.** The slide shows the punch line. The speaker delivers the setup (using Law 6 storytelling).
5. **Whitespace is your co-presenter.** Don't fill the slide. Let the idea breathe.

**Before:** "Our Comprehensive AI-Powered Data Intelligence Solution Enables Revenue Teams to Optimize Their Go-To-Market Strategy"

**After:** "Find the right buyers. Faster."

### Pitch Copy (for clf-meeting-prep / proposals)

**Framework: The P.A.R.A. Pitch Block**

Every section of a pitch document should follow this rhythm:

1. **Point:** Bold claim in one sentence. Level 1.
2. **Story:** One specific example — relived, not reported (Law 6). 2-4 sentences.
3. **Result:** The number. The outcome. The proof.
4. **Ask:** What you want them to do / believe / approve next.

**Example pitch paragraph:**
> Your outbound team is burning budget on bad data. [POINT]
>
> Last quarter, a SaaS company in your space was calling 300 contacts a day and getting 12 pickups. They switched to our verified lists. Same team, same scripts. 47 pickups a day within 30 days. [STORY + RESULT, relived]
>
> Let's run a 500-contact pilot and see what your numbers look like. [ASK]

### LinkedIn Posts / Social Copy

**Framework: The One-Thought Rule**

One post = one idea. Apply Law 4 (Whitespace) aggressively.

```
[Hook line — bold claim or surprising stat]

[2-3 lines expanding the point]

[The turn: the specific fact or result that changes the picture]

[The insight — the thing only you can say]

[CTA or question to invite engagement]
```

Line breaks between every 1-2 sentences. Social feeds are skimmed, not read.

## The Vinh Rewrite Checklist

Run this checklist on ANY piece of copy before delivering it:

| # | Check | Pass? |
|---|-------|-------|
| 1 | **Complexity Ladder:** Is every sentence at Level 1-2? Read each one aloud. | |
| 2 | **Banned word purge:** Search for all 25 banned words. Delete or replace every one. | |
| 3 | **Speed variation:** Do sentence lengths vary? Is there at least one 3-5 word sentence near a key point? | |
| 4 | **Whitespace:** Does any paragraph exceed 3 sentences? Break it up. | |
| 5 | **P.A.R.A. present:** Can you identify Point, Action, Result, Ask? | |
| 6 | **Story check:** Is there at least one specific, sensory, relived moment? (Law 6) | |
| 7 | **Meaning bridge:** Does every story connect to what it means for the reader? | |
| 8 | **The friend test:** Would you send this exact text to a smart friend who knows nothing about your industry? | |
| 9 | **One CTA:** Is there exactly one clear thing you want the reader to do? | |
| 10 | **Proof point:** Is there at least one specific number, name, or outcome? | |
| 11 | **No AI slop:** Ran `no-ai-slop` in Gate mode. Zero em dashes, no binary contrasts, no kicker endings. | |

## Calibration Guide: Matching Tone to Context

| Context | Complexity Level | Speed Pattern | Whitespace | Voice | Story Use |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|------------|-------|-----------|
| Cold email | 1 | Fast throughout | Aggressive breaks | Direct, human | One micro-story with a number |
| Follow-up email | 1-2 | Medium, builds to punch | Medium | Warm, persistent | "Here's what happened for X..." |
| Talking points | 1 | Speech rhythm, fragments OK | Mark pauses | Conversational | Anchor story per point |
| Slide titles | 1 | Punchy, 6-8 words max | Extreme | Bold, clear | None — story lives in notes |
| Speaker notes | 1-2 | Alternating fast/slow | Natural | Storytelling | Full relived story |
| Pitch document | 1-2 | Build to punch | Strategic | Confident, precise | One per section |
| Proposal / one-pager | 2 | Measured, steady | Moderate | Professional but human | Case study as narrative |
| LinkedIn post | 1-2 | Medium with punch ending | Very aggressive | Personal, opinionated | Personal anecdote |
| Internal comms | 1-2 | Natural | Normal | Friendly, direct | Optional |
| Battle card | 1 | Fast, scannable | Aggressive | Sharp, competitive | Competitor comparison story |

## Before/After Transformation Examples

### Example 1: Cold Outreach Email Opening

**Before (Level 3-4):**
> I'm reaching out to explore potential synergies between our organizations. Our comprehensive suite of data-driven solutions has been instrumental in facilitating the growth trajectories of numerous enterprise-level clients across verticals.

**After (Level 1, with Law 6 story):**
> Your team called 10,000 people last quarter. How many picked up? We helped Acme Corp go from 4% to 12% connect rate in 60 days. Same team. Better list.

### Example 2: Slide Title

**Before (Level 4):**
> Accelerate Your Digital Transformation Journey With Our AI-Powered Enterprise Intelligence Platform

**After (Level 1):**
> Find the right buyers. Faster.

### Example 3: Pitch Document Section

**Before (Level 4-5):**
> We are a leading provider of holistic, end-to-end business intelligence solutions, empowering organizations to leverage data-driven methodologies for optimized decision-making across the enterprise value chain.

**After (Level 1-2, with story):**
> A SaaS company in Austin was spending $40K/month on outbound. Half those calls went to wrong numbers. We cleaned their list in a week. The next month they booked 3x the meetings on the same budget.

**Before (Level 3):**
> I wanted to circle back regarding our previous conversation about how our innovative platform can help streamline your customer acquisition workflow.

**After (Level 1):**
> Quick update: since we last spoke, we ran a pilot with a company your size. They booked 23 meetings in their first month. Want to see the breakdown?

## Workflow

When this skill triggers (standalone or as a companion layer), follow this sequence:

1. **Identify the copy type** (email, slide, pitch, talking points, etc.) and select the matching playbook.
2. **Assess the current draft** (if one exists) against the Complexity Ladder. What level is it at?
3. **Run the Banned Word List** — search and replace.
4. **Apply the 6 Laws** — Complexity, Voice, Speed, Whitespace, P.A.R.A., Storytelling.
5. **Run the Vinh Rewrite Checklist** (all 10 items).
6. **Calibrate** using the Calibration Guide for the specific copy type.
7. **Run the friend test** one final time: read the output aloud. If any sentence sounds like a brochure instead of a human, rewrite that sentence.
8. **Deliver** the rewritten copy with a brief note on what changed and why (so the user learns the method, not just the output).

## Three Storytelling Misconceptions (from Vinh)

When someone says they "can't tell stories," they're usually stuck in one of these:

1. **"My life isn't interesting."** Stories aren't about events. Walking your dog can be a better story than climbing Everest — if the meaning is relatable. In copy: you don't need a dramatic case study. A small, specific result told well beats a vague big win.

2. **"Stories are just fluff."** Facts without stories are forgettable. Stories are the glue that makes facts stick to the brain. In copy: the data point lands harder when wrapped in a 2-sentence narrative.

3. **"I'm not a storyteller."** You don't need charisma. You need structure and specificity. The 3 ingredients (Specific, Relive, Meaning) are a formula anyone can follow.

## Final gate: no AI slop (mandatory before delivery)

Everything this skill produces that a person will read (client, prospect, vendor, partner, public, or the sales team) passes a no-AI-slop check before it is delivered. Load the `no-ai-slop` skill in Gate mode and run its Eval on the final copy. If that skill cannot be loaded, apply this minimum:

- Zero em dashes and en dashes anywhere, including headings, titles, subject lines, and date ranges. Use periods, commas, colons, parentheses, or restructure.
- Cut binary contrasts ("It's not X, it's Y", "Not because X. Because Y."), throat-clearing openers ("Here's the thing"), faux-insight setups ("What nobody tells you"), colon reveals ("The best part: it learns"), dramatic fragments ("That's it."), rhetorical setups, fake-profound kickers, and recap endings.
- Cut puffery and weasel attribution ("a testament to", "pivotal moment", "experts agree", "studies show"). Name the source or drop the claim. Never invent a source, stat, or quote.
- Banned words: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, seamless, unlock, synergy, game changer, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness, ever-evolving.
- Portability test: a sentence that could move unchanged to another company is filler. Replace it with a name, number, date, or mechanism, or cut it.
- Repeat the right word instead of cycling synonyms. Active voice, human subjects, direct verbs. No decorative bold or emoji headings.
- This gate governs style only. It never overrides this skill's factual, brand, or client-safety rules (vendor firewall, entity separation, verified numbers).
