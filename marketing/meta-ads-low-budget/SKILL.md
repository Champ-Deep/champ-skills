# Meta ads on a low budget

A planning and review playbook for small-budget Meta lead generation. Written for LakeB2B and Champions Yacht Club Goa, usable for any brand. It sits upstream of `nostalgia-angle-ads` (concepts) and `span-ad-creative` (render pipeline): this skill decides the offer, the funnel, the test and the readout; those two make the pictures.

| Check | Pass condition | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Is Meta the right channel for this buyer? | The buyer is a person who browses Instagram or Facebook and decides on their own, or a small decision-maker group reachable by broad targeting | Use LinkedIn or search. ## The six tactics

### 1. One specific, urgent offer with a hero mechanism (summary 0:50 to 2:03)

Pick a high-value product (the summary says $2,500 and up) and give it a mechanism that makes it different from the competitor set, not a discount. Write the offer in one sentence: who it is for, what they get, why it is only this way here, why now. Urgency must be real (capacity, a date, a cap), never invented.

### 2. Sell the report, not the call (2:07 to 5:11)

Send traffic to an opt-in page for a useful free guide, not a booking page. The creator uses a quick 12-page PDF, then a long-form page that acknowledges the download, states the offer and carries a simple screen-recorded pitch. He claims $10 to $25 a lead against about $250 a booked call; neither is verified for us. The list becomes an outbound calling list. His fallback, a Meta instant form, he rates as the lowest-quality lead, workable only with an instant call. The guide must be genuinely useful and contain only facts we can defend (`defensible-data-counts`: no buyer-visible number we cannot trace).

### 3. Test angles, not formats (5:26 to 8:30)

The video's Halo strategy: copy the long, emotionally charged comments from Reddit and from reviews of best-selling Amazon books in the niche, then pick the two angles that recur most and hurt most. At $30 a day he runs two angles and about three creatives, in two static styles (a breaking-news look and a raw phone photo), with a 45 to 50 character headline and story copy of 500 words or more. For our B2B brands swap Amazon for Trustpilot, G2 and other competitor reviews, plus permissioned call notes. Save short actual quotes with permalink, date/context and source type; deduplicate and report actual counts. Complaints are self-selected allegations, not facts or prevalence. Test two belief/pain angles in the same static format. Test breaking-news versus raw-native styling later with angle fixed; if both vary, label the result combined. Never fake news, testimonials or personal experiences.

### 4. Broad targeting, simple structure (10:16 to 11:16)

The video launches on ad-set budgets, one ad set per ad, gender and location only, optimised for leads, left alone for 7 days. He rejects a campaign budget (CBO) at launch because Meta piles spend onto one creative. No interest stacking by default. Broad stays within approved geography, language, age and eligibility; do not imply Meta verifies B2B job roles. Ad-set budgets can reserve spend for each angle. Separate ad sets can overlap and are directional comparisons, not randomized proof. Confirm current account capabilities and measurement before choosing the structure.

### 5. Use AI to read the data, then scale (11:28 to 14:24)

The video connects Meta's ads MCP to Claude, asks for a 7-day readout of winners and new angles, and lets it switch ads off, while warning that it makes mistakes. Ours is read-only: export ad and CRM outcomes, let AI identify anomalies and suggest tests, then a person rules. His scaling step comes only after winners exist: one CBO with 80 percent of budget holding the winners, 20 percent left on ad-set budgets for new creatives, two new angles or variations every two weeks, budget changes only on a 5-day window, kill any ad above twice his $15 lead target, and treat click-through under 1 percent as failing. Those thresholds fit his budget; set ours before launch. CBO and an 80 percent winning / 20 percent testing split are creator-proposed candidates, not an automatic move after two readouts. Require qualified-outcome evidence, attribution checks, conversion lag, an approved cap and Deep's explicit approval before changing budget. AI never changes the account.

### 6. Phone the lead inside 60 seconds (17:48 to 18:32)

For consented enquiries in staffed local calling hours, target a first attempt within 60 seconds and log attempted versus connected timestamps. Define after-hours handling and opt-out before launch. Ask why they opted in, record their language with permission and feed it into new angle hypotheses. A download is not unlimited calling consent. Verify caller identity and capacity, especially the paid media lead Majinder versus ads operator the paid media lead P.

### Also in the video, not in the pasted summary

- The unit sum (14:19 to 16:37): his example is a $2,500 offer, 40 leads, 15 percent booking, 60 percent showing, one close. Run the same sum with our own rates before setting any cap; if one close cannot pay for the test, the offer is wrong for this channel.
- House money (18:59 to 19:51): take the first stake back out once recovered and recycle the rest. A spending call for Deep, not a rule here.

## Build order (the checklist to run)

1. Fit gate passes. Offer sentence written and approved by the brand owner.
2. Angle research: 20 to 30 verbatim quotes from Reddit, reviews and sales calls, each with its source link. Cluster into pains and desires. Pick two angles that differ in the buyer's belief, not in wording.
3. Opt-in page and the guide: one page, one field set (name, phone or email as the follow-up needs), consent line, thank-you page with the turnaround. No page, no spend.
4. Creative: two angles by two looks is four ads at most for the first round (PROPOSED). Static, brand-compliant, no unverified claim, no counts. Load the brand skill, then `nostalgia-angle-ads` rules 6 to 8 for the image.
5. Account: broad audience, ad-set budgets, one ad set per ad (the video's structure), optimised for leads, no campaign budget until winners exist.
6. Tracking: UTM on every ad (source meta, campaign, angle, look), CRM Lead Source set at form submit, thank-you page event. The CRM is the source of truth (Q4 note: GA showed 282 and the CRM 198 for September, an 84 lead gap).
7. Phone: named caller, 60 second target, a one-page call script with the five questions below, call notes into the CRM.
8. Readout after a fixed window (see below), then scale or stop by a rule written before launch.

Five call questions: What made you sign up today? What have you tried so far? What almost stopped you? What would make this a clear yes? Who else decides?

## Readout rules (PROPOSED, set the numbers with the brand owner before launch)

- Judge on cost per sales-accepted lead and per closed deal, not cost per lead. A cheap lead nobody can reach is a loss.
- Write a budget-aware evidence threshold and review window before launch, including expected qualified-lead cost and conversion lag. No universal 30 to 50 leads or seven-day rule. If the cap cannot support the sample, report directional or inconclusive results; never exceed it to manufacture certainty.
- Write the stop rule first: total test cap, the cost per accepted lead that ends a test, the review date.
- Keep one variable per round. If both angle and look changed, the readout says "combined", not "angle".
- Record every call's top two phrases in the register; the next round's angles must cite at least one of them.

## Guardrails

- No spend is approved by this skill. Every plan ends in a stop rule and a decision for Deep.
- No claim in an ad that is not confirmed in writing by the brand owner (counts, turnaround, safety, fleet ownership, awards).
- No competitor names in our ad copy or opt-in pages. Competitor reviews are research only.
- Opt-in and calling consent are required before any outreach; stage legal review, do not assume.
- A model reads data and suggests; it never launches, pauses or scales a campaign.
