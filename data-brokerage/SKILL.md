# Data brokerage origination

## When to Use

Use this skill when the request involves positioning Champions Group as a data broker or originator between companies its teams already reach and buyers licensing operational data for AI: evaluating buyer partners, qualifying prospective data suppliers, mapping the brokerage motion onto existing outreach, drafting buyer or supplier outreach, and gating a first pilot. Do not use it for selling enrichment services or consuming external data feeds, those are different lanes.

Champions already runs SME and sector outreach (healthcare, technology). This skill adds a separate lane on that outreach: qualify approached companies as potential operational-data suppliers, match them to a buyer's written requirements, and introduce them under registered terms for a separate originator fee. The existing enrichment and lead-gen sales keep running unchanged beside it.

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the lane.** Three lanes never merge: licensing our own datasets, selling enrichment to clients, and brokering other companies' operational data. State which lane a request touches before acting on it; everything below is the third lane only.
2. **Get a written buyer brief before sourcing anything.** Active datasets wanted, acceptance criteria, exclusions, secure-review process, commercial arrangement. No brief means the next call goes to the buyer, not to any supplier: sourcing against imagined demand produces qualified companies nobody buys.
3. **Evaluate buyers on published evidence, labeled honestly.** In any partner table keep three categories distinct: first-party published facts, vendor marketing claims, and our proposed or unconfirmed terms. A referral page existing proves the route, not the economics; a payout figure is a vendor claim until a contract says it. Confirm the intermediary arrangement itself in writing (fee basis, payout trigger, duplicate and renewal handling, exclusivity, entity and tax eligibility), not just the program's existence. Check attribution mechanics before introducing anyone: some programs refuse retroactive credit, so register first.
4. **Map into the existing process as a proposed overlay.** Read the Lead Gen Workspace stages (Signal through Retro, `Celsus/Efforts/Lead Gen Workspace/CONTEXT.md`) and propose additions per stage: a supplier-fit signal, a metadata-only inventory, a supplier-fit column beside commercial fit, a separate opt-in thread, rights confirmation with owner plus IT/data plus legal, written lead registration, and retro tracking of payout and rejection reasons. Propose only; never edit stages, CRM fields, campaigns, or automations as part of research or planning.
5. **Qualify the supplier metadata-only.** Systems, record types, date ranges, record counts, owner identity. No raw payload, no credentials; nothing leaves the owner's environment until legal scope is signed. Contact the founder or COO, the IT or data owner, and legal or privacy, not only the CMO.
6. **Run a separate opt-in discovery thread.** Transparent about the originator role, one CTA, batches of 25, no pretext, no earnings promises. Enrichment or marketing permission is not AI-licensing permission: never reuse consent gathered for lead work as consent to license records.
7. **Get terms written before any introduction.** Lead registration, duplicate handling, payout trigger and timeline, renewals, exclusivity, tax eligibility. Then match the metadata inventory to the named brief and introduce.
8. **Apply sector rules.** Technology first: operationally mature, primarily English, matched to a confirmed brief. Healthcare starts with non-patient operational material (SOPs, workflows, support handling); clinical or patient-adjacent corpora go only through a specialist partner on a legal and privacy pathway, never through normal intake. Existing sales disqualifiers do not carry over: a company too small or too unfocused to buy leads can still hold valuable records.
9. **Gate the first pilot.** One buyer, one supplier at a time. Go only with written intermediary terms in hand, supplier opt-in with named owner and legal/privacy contacts, a clean rights check (no PHI, no unlicensed third-party content, de-identification defined owner-side), a metadata inventory matching a live brief, registration cleared against duplicates and brand suppression, and payout eligibility for our entity. Stop if the buyer will not put terms in writing, the supplier wants us to hold raw records, or the rights check fails. Count success only when an originator fee is actually received; track time, cost, and rejection reasons so a signed deal is not mistaken for a profitable program.

## Standing rules

- Never present a partnership, fee, licence, or partner acceptance as established before a signed source says so, in any internal or external document.
- Draft outreach may assert only states that already exist; frame everything unagreed as a request or question.
- Own-dataset licensing, client enrichment, and brokerage never pool datasets, rights, or money.
- Current partner research and the worked stage overlay live in `Celsus/Efforts/Active/Data Licensing Partnerships/`; re-read before quoting partner names, since rankings and terms change.
