# Pillar 4: Landing Page Strategy

A product landing page has one job: build enough trust, fast enough, that a person tries the product. AI defaults reach for abstraction (a grid of generic feature icons, a vague gradient hero) precisely when concreteness is what persuades.

## Contents
- Presentation over complexity
- Show the real product
- Trust scaffolding

---

## Presentation over complexity

The instinct to explain everything works against you. A landing page is not documentation. It is a first impression, and first impressions are visual and emotional before they are informational.

The shift: **lead with presentation, not feature count.** One strong, specific visual that shows the product doing its job beats a wall of icons and copy. Complexity can live one click deeper, on a features page or in docs.

What this looks like in practice:
- A hero that states the value in plain words and shows the product, not a metaphor.
- Generous whitespace and a calm palette (the same considered palette from Pillar 1), so the product visual is the loudest thing on the page.
- One primary call to action, repeated, not five competing buttons.

---

## Show the real product

The highest-trust asset you have is the product itself. Use **stylized, high-quality screenshots of your actual analytics dashboard** as the hero and section visuals, rather than illustrative icons that could belong to any company.

How to make product screenshots look premium:
- **Frame them.** Put the screenshot in a soft browser or device frame, on a subtle background, with a gentle shadow so it sits in space.
- **Crop to the moment.** Show the one view that proves value (the analytics with a real trend, the map with live data), not the whole cluttered app.
- **Use real, flattering data.** Numbers that look plausible and healthy. Never lorem-ipsum placeholders or obviously fake "123" values.
- **Annotate sparingly.** A single callout pointing at the one feature that matters helps. Ten callouts is noise.
- **Tilt or stack for depth.** A slight perspective or a layered stack of two screens reads as designed without hiding the content.

This is the specific point from the source material: stylized screenshots of your real dashboard show value immediately, where a row of generic feature icons shows nothing. People believe what they can see working.

## Trust scaffolding

Around the product visual, add the proof that lowers risk:
- **Specific value proposition** in the headline. Concrete beats clever. Say what it does and for whom.
- **Social proof** near the hero: logos, a real metric ("3.2M links tracked"), or a short testimonial with a name and face.
- **A second visual section** that shows a different real view (for example the link list and the creation modal), reinforcing that the product is complete.
- **Honest, legible pricing entry** that links to the humane billing layout from Pillar 3.
- **One consistent call to action** all the way down the page.

---

## Quick wins, in order
1. Replace the generic icon hero with a framed, stylized screenshot of the real dashboard.
2. Rewrite the headline to a specific value proposition and collapse to one primary call to action.
3. Add one row of social proof (logos or a real metric) near the hero.
4. Add a second product-screenshot section showing a different real view, with realistic data.
