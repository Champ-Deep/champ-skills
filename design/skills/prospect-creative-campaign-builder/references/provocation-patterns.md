# Provocation Patterns

## The structure

A provocation is a claim the reader currently believes, stated in their own
words, then contradicted with something they can check. It is not a hot take.

1. **Name the assumption.** "Most teams think X." Use the reader's phrasing,
   not the vendor's.
2. **Contradict it with a number or a mechanism.** Prefer a mechanism they can
   reason about over a statistic they must trust.
3. **Show the cost of the current belief**, in money, time, or risk, and make
   it near-term. A cost three years out is not a provocation, it is a forecast.
4. **Offer the reframe** as a question the reader can answer themselves.

## Patterns that work

- **The inversion:** "You are measuring {metric}. It moves the opposite way to
  the outcome you actually want."
- **The borrowed authority:** an adjacent industry that already hit this wall.
- **The arithmetic check:** show the multiplication the reader has never done.
- **The false consensus:** "Everyone assumes this is standard. It is not."
- **The missing line item:** the cost nobody puts in the spreadsheet.

## Failure modes

- Provoking without evidence. The reader's first reaction must be "is that
  true?", not "say who".
- Provoking the reader's own decision. Aim at the market's assumption, not at
  the person you want to buy.
- Hedging. "Some might argue" deflates the whole device. State it or cut it.

A provocation earns the right to a recommendation. Land it, then stop talking.
