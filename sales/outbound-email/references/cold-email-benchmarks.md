# Cold Email & Campaign Benchmarks (evidence file)

Evidence behind every word-count, subject-length, send-window, follow-up, CTA and volume rule in these skills. Last consolidated 2026-10-14.

## Sources and how to read them

| Source | Dataset | Denominator note |
|---|---|---|
| r/internships study (u/Key_Reputation_4690, May 2026), full breakdown at whali.co.uk/blog/cold-email-data-study-students-2026, popularized by Edward Sturm (youtube.com/shorts/zF_XIBi0SJM) | 6,337 emails, 200+ students, Nov 2025 to May 2026 | Student internship outreach, not B2B. Open rates inflated 30 to 40% by Apple Mail Privacy Protection |
| Instantly 2026 Benchmark Report | Platform-wide, billions of sends | Average reply 3.43%, down from 8.5% in 2019 |
| Belkins 2026 study (belkins.io, Jun 2026) | 7.5M cold emails, 2025, replies divided by total sends | Strictest denominator: 0.45% average. Different method from every platform benchmark, do not compare directly |
| Smartlead benchmarks (smartlead.ai/benchmarks) | 850M+ emails | Per-step and per-day/hour reply rates |
| lemlist (how-many-cold-email-follow-ups) | Millions of campaigns | Per-email and cumulative reply rates |
| Woodpecker 2026 cold email statistics | 20M+ emails | Personalization, list size, sequence length |
| Backlinko email outreach study | 12M emails | Personalization lift, follow-up lift |
| Autobound 2026 guide | Aggregates Instantly, Belkins, Martal | Personalization ladder |

Reply-rate averages across sources range from 0.45% (Belkins, strict cold, net-new, against total sends) to 3.43% (Instantly platform average) to 8.5% (older 2019-era reports). Good campaigns sit at 5 to 10%, signal-personalized campaigns at 15%+. Always know which denominator produced the number you are quoting.

## Word count: one clear consensus

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| 6,337-email study | Under 100 words: 11.9% reply. 100 to 200: 1.9%. 200 to 300: 0.3% |
| 3-million-email study (howlongshouldacoldemailbe.com) | 50 to 125 words: 2.4x the reply rate of 200+ word emails |
| Instantly | Keep first touches under 80 words |
| Boomerang | 50 to 125 words responds best |
| lemlist | About 120 words: 52% booking rate. 300 words: 20% |
| Whali | 3 to 5 sentences: 3.6% average reply, the highest band |

Rule stands: under 100 words target, 125 hard cap, 3 to 5 sentences. No source argues for longer.

## Subject length: consensus with one contested claim

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| 6,337-email study | Under 30 characters: 83.7% open. 70+: 69.5%. Contains a number: 80.6%, highest pattern |
| Belkins B2B subject statistics | 2 to 4 word subjects: 46% open, the highest band |
| Yesware (262,518 templates) | Open rates peak at 1 to 5 words |
| Marketo | 41 characters / 7 words is the sweet spot |
| Contra (2025 data) | 20 to 40 characters, target about 41 |
| Mailchimp guidance | Max 9 words, 60 characters |
| Mailmend | Single-word subjects average 38% open, below the 2 to 4 word band. Beyond 7 words open rates fall to 39%, at 10 words 34% |
| MarketingSherpa | In their dataset, subject length had no correlation with opens (Pearson -0.03). 61 to 70 characters had the highest read rate |
| Attentive (billions of subjects, Cyber Week) | Under 25 characters won for opens and revenue efficiency, e-commerce only |

The Short's claim that one-word subjects get the best open rates traces to the First 1000 newsletter, and is contradicted by at least one dataset (single-word at 38% versus 2 to 4 words at 46%, reading as automated). Safe synthesis: 2 to 4 words, roughly 15 to 40 characters, natural and specific, a number in the subject is worth testing. One-word subjects are an A/B variant, never the default rule. Subject length is a weaker lever than word count and personalization, so do not over-tune it.

## Follow-ups: the contested zone

Per-email reply rate declines at every step. This is agreed across every dataset:

| Step | Smartlead (850M) | lemlist (millions) |
|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | 1.18% | 4.5% |
| Email 2 | 0.73% (62% of step 1) | 3.75% |
| Email 3 | 0.56% (47%) | 2.75% |
| Email 4 | 0.49% (42%) | 2.1% |
| Email 7 | 0.33% (28%) | still climbing |

Whether cumulative replies keep climbing past email three is where sources split:

| Source | Position |
|---|---|
| 6,337-email study | Peaks at 2 follow-ups (3 emails total): 6.94% cumulative, then 5.75% at 4. Past two follow-ups, "you're spamming" |
| Whali / Saleshandy (100M+) | Same shape: 6.64% at 2 emails, 6.94% peak at 3, down 17% at 4. But 42% of all replies come from follow-ups, not email 1 |
| Instantly | Reply rates drop 55% by the fourth follow-up |
| Woodpecker (20M) | 4 to 7 touchpoints optimal: 8.3% reply versus 4.1% with no follow-up. First follow-up is often the best single step at 8.4% |
| Smartlead | Cumulative replies per 1,000 contacts: 12 (1 email), 19 (2), 25 (3), 30 (4), 33 (5), 40 (7). Follow-ups bring 71% of replies at 7 emails |
| lemlist | Cumulative reply rate climbs to 22.37% at 10 emails. Recommends 4 to 9 follow-ups |
| Backlinko (12M) | One follow-up alone generates 65.8% more replies than email 1 on its own |

Synthesis: three emails (first touch plus two follow-ups) is the floor every dataset supports and the right default for a new or reputation-sensitive prospecting domain. Larger platform datasets show additional gains through 4 to 7 touches, but with shrinking per-step yield (each step earns about 60% then 47% then 42% of the step before), and the smaller controlled studies show a drop after three. The binding constraint is not reply count, it is sender reputation: Google requires user-reported spam under 0.1% and never 0.3%, and unsubscribe spikes are what made the student dataset go negative past two follow-ups. If a fourth email is sent, it must carry a genuinely new angle or asset, never a bump, and channel-switching (LinkedIn, phone, WhatsApp) beats a longer email chain every time. Belkins confirms: LinkedIn follow-ups after email runs dry clear 7%+ reply rates versus continuing a dead thread.

## Send window: day matters little, hour and timezone matter more

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Smartlead (850M) | Every weekday falls between 0.87% and 0.91%, a spread of 0.04 points. Wednesday highest at 0.91%, Saturday lowest at 0.82%, Sunday 0.90% level with Tuesday. By reply rate, day of week is nearly noise |
| Smartlead hours | 7 AM recipient-local: 1.34%. Every hour 6 to 10 AM: 1.17% or better. 4 PM: 0.82%. Afternoon 1 to 5 PM: 0.79 to 0.86% |
| Belkins 7.5M | Wednesday and Thursday best at 0.48% each. Morning 8 AM to noon best at 0.54%. Evening sends, the winner in prior years, now rank LAST at 0.40% |
| WarmySender (75k B2B, Jul 2025 to Jan 2026) | Tuesday 9 to 11 AM local: 4.8%. Weekdays 4.1% versus weekends 1.6% (61% drop) |
| ReachIQ (millions) | 8 to 10 AM bucket: 5.6% median reply. Best versus worst window spreads 1.5 to 2.5 points, versus 3 to 5 points from personalization |
| Snov.io | Highest opens Tuesday 28.2% and Wednesday 27.5%. Response most likely 7 to 11 AM |

Synthesis: send first touches on weekday mornings, 8 to 11 AM in the RECIPIENT's timezone. Tuesday to Thursday is slightly ahead and weekends and Friday afternoon are clearly worse, but day-of-week effects are small next to hour and timezone. Morning displaced evening as the winning slot in 2025 data; "midday to early afternoon" (carried from the student study's UTC hours) is not supported by B2B datasets. Timing is a multiplier on research quality, never a substitute for it.

## CTA type: soft asks beat meeting asks, badly

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Puzzle Inbox (200k+ cold emails) | Soft CTA: 4.2% reply. Medium: 3.1%. Hard (book a meeting): 1.4% |
| Saleshandy (53M) | A single soft CTA generates 78% more positive replies than hard CTAs |
| Cleverly / ColdICP | Calendar link in first touch kills replies; yes/no problem-confirmation questions consistently win first touch |
| r/startups operator data | Switching from meeting push to yes/no questions: 2% to 10%+ reply rate |

Confirms the existing skill rules: permission-ask or binary yes/no in Touch 1, no calendar link until interest is shown, one CTA only.

## Personalization depth (the biggest lever)

| Level | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| None (batch template) | 1 to 3% |
| Basic (first name, company, title) | 5 to 9% |
| Advanced (industry pain, recent news) | 9 to 15%, Woodpecker measured 17 to 18% versus 7 to 9% basic |
| Signal-based (specific trigger plus tailored value) | 15 to 25% |
| Multi-signal stacked (2 to 3 layered signals) | 25 to 40% |

Backlinko (12M): personalized bodies get 32.7% more responses. Autobound: an email sent within 24 to 48 hours of a buying trigger gets 3 to 5x the response rate regardless of day or time. 78% of decision-makers respond more when the email proves understanding of their business. Every layer of genuine signal roughly doubles the rate. This is why research time beats send-time tuning.

## List size: smaller and verified wins

| Recipients per campaign | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Under 50 | 5.8% |
| 51 to 250 | 4.6% |
| 251 to 1,000 | 3.2% |
| 1,000+ | 2.1% |

Verified lists earn about 2x the reply rate of unverified ones (Woodpecker, Cleanlist 2026). Applies directly to how lead-gen playbooks size a run.

## Who actually replies (Belkins 7.5M)

Founders and owners 0.57%, C-level 0.42%, VPs 0.32% (lowest). Companies with 0 to 10 employees reply at 0.72%, 10,000+ at 0.22%. Directors and budget-holding ICs beat reflexive C-suite targeting except at genuine enterprises. Corroborates the student study's finding that Directors and Managers get 3x more outreach than CEOs and reply better at firms over 50 people.

## Format and infrastructure guardrails

- Plain text beats HTML for cold: 12 to 20% higher reply rates on a 12M-email analysis; HTML cold emails bounce 674% more than plain text (Hunter). Confirms the plain-text rule.
- Volume: 30 to 50 cold emails per warmed inbox per day, hard cap about 100, new inboxes 10 to 20 during 2 to 4 weeks of warmup, no cold sends in the first two weeks. Scale by adding inboxes and domains, never by pushing one mailbox.
- Google sender rules: user-reported spam under 0.10%, never reach 0.30%. Bounce rate under 2%. These thresholds, not reply math, are the hard stop on sequence length.

## What this changes in the skills

1. Send window corrected from "midday to early afternoon" to weekday mornings 8 to 11 AM recipient-local, day-of-week treated as a minor factor.
2. One-word subject demoted from "tests best" to an A/B variant; 2 to 4 words / 15 to 40 characters is the default.
3. Follow-up cap reframed: three emails stays the default and the floor, but the "past two follow-ups you're spamming" claim is one camp of a contested split; per-step yield decline, spam thresholds and channel-switching are the real constraints.
4. Confirmed without change: under 100 word first touches, plain text, one link, soft yes/no CTA, no calendar link before reply, signal-first research, founders/directors over reflexive C-suite.
5. Added for playbooks: personalization ladder as calibration, list-size table, per-inbox volume caps, spam and bounce thresholds.
