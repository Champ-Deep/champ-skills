# Pitch Frameworks

Two primary frameworks. Select based on trigger type (see SKILL.md Step 5).
Each framework covers: structure, subject line rules, and a fully annotated example for each touch.

---

## Framework A — Signal-Based Challenger

**Use when:** A specific, verifiable behavioural trigger exists (hiring, funding, tech change, expansion).
**Core mechanic:** Acknowledge the trigger → challenge a common assumption → offer a proprietary insight → ask for permission to share.
**Why it works:** Proves research depth in the first sentence. Positions sender as peer, not vendor. Zero-friction CTA.

---

### Touch 1 — Cold Email

**Structure:**
1. **The Signal** (1 sentence): Name the specific trigger. Be precise — company name + what happened.
2. **The Challenge** (2–3 sentences): State the common assumption people make given that trigger. Then introduce a counter-intuitive reality — the thing most people don't realise until it's too late.
3. **The Proof** (1–2 sentences): Name a comparable outcome achieved for a similar company. Metric-specific. No company name needed.
4. **The Ask** (1 sentence): Binary yes/no. Offer to send an asset (one-pager, breakdown, audit findings). No link.

**Subject line rules:**
- Format: `[Company Name] + [short trigger reference]` (under 7 words total)
- Never use: "Quick question", "Following up", "Touching base", "Partnership opportunity"
- Good examples: `Acme Corp + APAC expansion` / `Tekflow's Salesforce migration` / `Idea for Meridian's Q2 pipeline`

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Acme Corp + APAC expansion

Hi Sarah,

Noticed the recent hiring push for outbound leadership across your APAC division.

Most RevOps leaders scaling into new regions assume doubling headcount doubles pipeline — 
but find that localised data compliance and domain reputation limits actually throttle 
outreach velocity within 60 days of launch.

We recently helped a similar enterprise bypass this with a DPDP-compliant data rotation 
strategy that increased their meeting-booked rate by 14% without adding headcount.

I wrote up a quick one-page breakdown of the exact infrastructure setup — worth sending over?

Best,
[Name]
```

*Why it works: Signal is specific (APAC hiring push). Challenge reframes their assumption (headcount ≠ pipeline). Proof is metric-anchored. CTA is a binary, low-friction permission ask with zero links.*

---

### Touch 2 — Follow-Up (Day 5–7)

**Structure:**
1. **Re: thread** — always reply to original subject line to maintain context.
2. **1-sentence recap** of core value prop.
3. **The Easy Out** — explicitly give them permission to say not now. This reduces pressure and paradoxically increases reply rate.
4. **The Ask** — simple yes/no on current priority.

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Re: Acme Corp + APAC expansion

Hi Sarah,

Sent a note last week about your APAC expansion and how data compliance tends to cap 
outreach velocity earlier than most teams expect.

If this isn't a priority for the current quarter, completely understood.

If it is on the roadmap — is it worth a quick conversation next week?

Best,
[Name]
```

*Why it works: No guilt, no "just checking in." Easy out disarms defensiveness. Tight word count respects their time.*

---

### Touch 3 — Breakup (Day 12–14)

**Structure:**
1. Brief acknowledgement this is the last note.
2. Leave the door fully open — warm, not resentful.
3. One soft final question or explicit close.

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Re: Acme Corp + APAC expansion

Hi Sarah,

I'll leave it here — I don't want to clog your inbox.

If the data infrastructure side of your APAC push becomes relevant further down the line, 
happy to pick this up then.

Worth keeping in touch?

Best,
[Name]
```

## Framework B — Value-First Asynchronous Audit

**Use when:** Operational inefficiency signal detected, or no strong single trigger — best for data providers, SaaS, agencies.
**Core mechanic:** Reference an operational observation → prove value with a comparable outcome → offer a free, no-commitment audit → ask for a specific time or permission to share findings.
**Why it works:** Shifts power dynamic from "pitch" to "give." Prospect receives value regardless of purchase. Drastically reduces perceived risk.

---

### Touch 1 — Cold Email

**Structure:**
1. **The Observation** (1 sentence): Reference something specific about their current operations — technographic data, vertical they're entering, recent activity.
2. **The Pattern** (1–2 sentences): Name the common problem that emerges in this situation (bounce rate creep, CPL inflation, sourcing delays). Frame as industry-wide, not a criticism of them specifically.
3. **The Proof** (1–2 sentences): Specific, metric-grounded outcome from a comparable company.
4. **The Offer** (1 sentence): Free, asynchronous audit — emphasise it provides value whether or not they buy.
5. **The Ask** (1 sentence): Specific time proposal (Tuesday afternoon / Wednesday morning) — no calendar link.

**Subject line rules:**
- Format: `Idea for [Company Name]'s [specific area]`
- Good examples: `Idea for Tekflow's data hygiene` / `Idea for Meridian's campaign targeting` / `Idea for Acme's recruiter pipeline`

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Idea for Acme Corp's data hygiene

Hi James,

I was looking at your recent expansion into healthcare SaaS — specifically the platform 
integrations you've rolled out.

When B2B teams scale into new verticals, bounce rates tend to creep past 5% within the 
first month, usually from stale vertical-specific data rather than sequencing issues.

We recently helped a med-tech team reduce their bounce rate from 6.2% to 0.8% in two 
weeks by overlaying real-time intent validation on their existing lists.

I can run a quick complimentary deliverability audit on your current setup — whether we 
work together or not, I'll share 2–3 fixes you can apply this week.

Does Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning work for a 10-minute walkthrough?

Best,
[Name]
```

*Why it works: Observation is specific to their vertical move. Problem is framed as industry-wide norm. Proof is ultra-specific. Audit offer removes risk entirely. Specific times eliminate scheduling friction without a calendar link.*

---

### Touch 2 — Follow-Up (Day 5–7)

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Re: Idea for Acme Corp's data hygiene

Hi James,

I sent a note last week offering a quick deliverability audit given your move into 
healthcare SaaS.

If data hygiene isn't a current priority, no problem at all.

If it is — is next week still workable for a brief look?

Best,
[Name]
```

---

### Touch 3 — Breakup (Day 12–14)

**Annotated Example:**

```
Subject: Re: Idea for Acme Corp's data hygiene

Hi James,

I'll stop reaching out — I know things get buried.

The audit offer stands whenever the timing works better on your end.

Best,
[Name]
```

## Post-Reply Protocol (applies to both frameworks)

Once a prospect replies with any degree of interest, the deliverability risk drops sharply — the algorithm treats the sender as trusted. Only at this point can scheduling links be introduced.

**Confirmed interest reply template:**

```
Hi [First Name],

Great — I can walk you through it on Tuesday at 2 PM or Thursday at 10 AM.

Which works better? I'll send the calendar link once you confirm.

Best,
[Name]
```

*Note: Propose specific times first, withhold the calendar link until they confirm. This maintains conversational tone and avoids triggering spam filters.*
