# Persona Matrix — Tone Rules by Title Tier × Industry Vertical

Use this file during Phase 2, Step 6 to calibrate copy tone, word choice, CTA style, and angle.
Apply **title tier rules first**, then **layer vertical modifiers on top**.

---

## Title Tier Rules

### Tier 1 — C-Suite (CEO, CFO, CTO, CMO, CPO, CISO, CRO)

**Tone:** Peer-to-peer, direct, strategic. Never subservient.
**What they care about:** Revenue impact, competitive risk, regulatory exposure, board-level optics.
**What they ignore:** Tactical features, process detail, anything that reads like a vendor pitch.
**Word count:** Lean to the shorter end (50–80 words). Their time is the scarcest.
**CTA style:** "Worth a quick look?" or "Happy to share the one-pager if useful." — never "book a demo."
**Opener pattern:** Start with the business consequence, not the product.
**Avoid:** Any hint of desperation, over-explanation, jargon, or flattery.

> Example opener (good): "Most RevOps leaders scaling into APAC find their outreach velocity caps out within 60 days — not from headcount, but from domain reputation limits."
> Example opener (bad): "I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out about our exciting data solutions..."

---

### Tier 2 — VP / Director Level (VP Sales, Director of Marketing, Head of Growth, etc.)

**Tone:** Consultative, data-led, slightly warmer than C-suite.
**What they care about:** Team performance metrics, hitting targets, operational efficiency, defending budget spend.
**What they ignore:** High-level strategy fluff, anything that doesn't map to their KPIs.
**Word count:** 75–110 words. More room for one specific metric or proof point.
**CTA style:** Specific time proposal ("Does Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning work?") or audit offer.
**Opener pattern:** Reference a specific operational signal (hiring, tool change, campaign activity).
**Avoid:** Buzzwords, claims without numbers, vague "we help companies like yours" openers.

> Example opener (good): "Noticed your team recently switched to Salesforce — most VP Sales teams we speak to find the first 90 days post-migration is where pipeline data quality degrades fastest."

---

### Tier 3 — Manager / Team Lead (Sales Manager, Marketing Manager, Demand Gen Manager, etc.)

**Tone:** Practical, empathetic, problem-first. Speak their daily frustrations.
**What they care about:** Making their team's job easier, hitting weekly/monthly targets, not getting burned by bad tools.
**What they ignore:** Strategic framing, executive language, ROI that feels abstract.
**Word count:** 90–125 words. More room to describe the specific problem clearly.
**CTA style:** Audit offer or "I can send over a quick breakdown — would that be useful?"
**Opener pattern:** Name a specific recurring operational headache that signals you understand their day.
**Avoid:** C-suite language ("board-level", "strategic imperative"), abstract revenue claims.

> Example opener (good): "When SDR teams start hitting new geo targets, bounce rates tend to creep past 5% within the first month — usually from stale regional data rather than bad sequencing."

---

### Tier 4 — Individual Contributor (SDR, BDR, Account Executive, Recruiter, etc.)

**Tone:** Friendly, direct, peer-to-peer. Very practical, zero corporate speak.
**What they care about:** Making their own quota/targets, tools that save them time, not looking bad in front of their manager.
**Word count:** 50–80 words. Keep it tight and actionable.
**CTA style:** Simple yes/no. "Worth a 10-minute look?"
**Note:** Individual contributors rarely have budget authority. If targeting ICs, frame the offer as something they can champion upward. Include a line like "could be worth flagging to your team."
**Avoid:** Executive-speak, large numbers without context, anything that requires approval to act on.

## Industry Vertical Modifiers

Layer these on top of the title tier base rules.

### Technology / SaaS

- **Angle:** Speed and scale — they move fast, expect vendors to too.
- **Proof point style:** Product metrics, activation rates, conversion data.
- **Tone modifier:** Confident, slightly technical. Can reference stack specifics.
- **Compliance sensitivity:** Medium. Mention GDPR/CCPA briefly if relevant, don't dwell.
- **Avoid:** Anything that sounds "old school" or manual.

---

### Healthcare / Pharma

- **Angle:** Compliance-first, then efficiency. They are risk-averse by default.
- **Proof point style:** Risk reduction, audit trail, regulatory coverage (HIPAA, DPDP, GDPR).
- **Tone modifier:** Formal, measured. Slightly more conservative than SaaS.
- **Compliance sensitivity:** Very high. Always mention compliance credentials if pitching data.
- **Avoid:** Aggressive urgency language. Never imply cutting corners.

---

### Financial Services

- **Angle:** Risk and ROI in equal measure. They want proof, not promises.
- **Proof point style:** Specific ROI figures, regulatory compliance (SOC 2, GDPR), security posture.
- **Tone modifier:** Professional, precise. Short sentences. No fluff.
- **Compliance sensitivity:** Very high. Mention data governance and auditability.
- **Avoid:** Vague claims, casual language at C-suite tier, anything that sounds speculative.

---

### Manufacturing / Industrial

- **Angle:** Operational efficiency and lead time reduction.
- **Proof point style:** Cost savings, time saved, headcount efficiency.
- **Tone modifier:** Direct, plain-spoken. Less comfortable with digital marketing language.
- **Compliance sensitivity:** Low–Medium. Focus on operational outcomes, not data theory.
- **Avoid:** SaaS-speak ("product-led growth", "PLG", "activation"), abstract tech references.

---

### Marketing / Agencies

- **Angle:** Client outcomes and campaign performance — they sell on results, so pitch on results.
- **Proof point style:** CPL, conversion rates, campaign ROI, audience precision.
- **Tone modifier:** Creative, energetic, but still data-grounded. Peer-to-peer.
- **Compliance sensitivity:** Medium. They care about deliverability and list quality acutely.
- **Avoid:** Anything that sounds like you're competing with them. Position as infrastructure, not strategy.

---

## Quick Combination Examples

| Persona | Tone Profile |
|---|---|
| CTO × SaaS | Ultra-direct, technical credibility, zero fluff, 60–75 words |
| VP Marketing × Healthcare | Consultative + compliance-anchored, proof point required, 90 words |
| Sales Manager × Financial Services | Problem-first, precise metrics, formal but not stiff, 100 words |
| CMO × Marketing Agency | Peer-to-peer, results-led, creative opener OK, 75–90 words |
| Director of TA × Manufacturing | Plain-spoken, efficiency angle, practical CTA, 90–110 words |
