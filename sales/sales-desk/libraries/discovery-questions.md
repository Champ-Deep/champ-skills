# Discovery Questions Library

Forty discovery questions in four groups with a when-to-use note per group, for prepping a first call or unstick a stalled one.

## Give Claude
- Optional: the account, contact and what they do, so the questions get ordered for your call
- Optional: notes from previous calls, so answered questions can be skipped
- Optional: which groups to use, if you only want one or two

## Claude does
1. Select from the bank below: ten situation, ten problem, ten implication, ten need-payoff questions, numbered 1 to 40.
2. If account details were pasted, reorder so your situation comes first and mark the five to ask this call.
3. Mark any question already answered in your notes as skip, with what was said instead.
4. Keep the wording as written unless you asked for it to be tailored to the account.

## Output
Four sections in this shape: group name, when-to-use line, then ten numbered questions. The bank:
### Situation: use when you are still establishing facts. Move on once answers stop adding new information or they say "not sure" twice.
1. How is this handled today, from start to finish?
2. Who owns that process right now?
3. How many people are involved each week?
4. Which tools does it run on today?
5. How long does one full cycle take?
6. How often does it happen?
7. What made you take this call now?
8. What have you tried so far?
9. When did this move up your priority list?
10. How do you measure whether it is working?
### Problem: use when they describe the current state comfortably. Move on as soon as one concrete pain gets named twice.
11. Where does it break most often?
12. What is the most frustrating part for your team?
13. What happens downstream when it goes wrong?
14. How often does that happen?
15. What workaround do people use?
16. Which step do people avoid or delay?
17. Where do handoffs between teams fail?
18. What have you tried that did not stick?
19. Who feels this problem most?
20. What would you change first?
### Implication: use when a pain is confirmed but still feels small. Move on when they state a cost in their own words.
21. What does that failure cost you in a typical month?
22. How does it reach the customer at the end?
23. What does your team do instead when it fails?
24. How does this show up for your manager or board?
25. What opportunities get missed because of it?
26. What effect does it have on hiring or retention?
27. What does it do to timelines on other projects?
28. If nothing changes, where are you in a year?
29. What is the risk of fixing this badly?
30. How does this compare with what your competitors can do?
### Need-payoff: use when the pain carries weight. Move on when they describe the future state without you prompting it.
31. If this worked smoothly, what would change for your team?
32. What would you do with the time you got back?
33. Who else would benefit, and how?
34. What would you put the saved money toward?
35. How would you judge success in the first quarter?
36. What would make this an easy decision to approve?
37. Which outcome matters most to you personally?
38. What happens if we solve only part of it?
39. What would your ideal version look like in six months?
40. What do you need from me to move this forward?

## Rules
- Forty questions, ten per group, no duplicates or rewordings of the same question.
- Each question must be askable aloud unchanged, in plain words.
- No leading questions that embed the answer you want.
- When-to-use lines describe a moment in the conversation, not internal stage names.
- Do not add a fifth group; if you drop a group, say which and why.
- Questions marked skip stay listed with the note, so nothing gets asked twice.
