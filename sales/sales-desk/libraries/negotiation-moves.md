# Negotiation Moves

Ten negotiation moves with the line to use and the moment to use it, for prepping a pricing call or handling a discount request live.

## Give Claude
- Optional: what they asked for (discount, terms, timing), pasted exactly
- Optional: what you are allowed to trade and your walk-away point
- Optional: deal context: size, stage, competition, who else is in the deal

## Claude does
1. Match the situation to the right move in the bank below and output the line to say, in your words.
2. Name the move you must not make here, for example conceding without a trade, and why.
3. Give the order to use moves if the conversation goes badly, three moves in sequence.
4. If no move fits, say so and give the fallback: pause, get the facts, agree a next call.
5. List what you are buying with any trade you propose, in one line.

## Output
- Move to use now: name, the line, what it buys you, when to say it.
- Banned move for this situation with the reason.
- Three-move sequence if it goes badly, in order.
- Bank below: ten moves, each with when to use it, the line, and what it trades.

### The bank
1. Trade, never concede. Use whenever they ask for something: give only against something you get. Line: "I can do that if we move to annual terms."
2. Anchor first. Use before they name a number: state yours with its logic. Line: "Based on the scope we discussed, this sits at your number. What did you have in mind?"
3. Silence. Use after their counter: say nothing for five slow seconds, then ask a question. Line: "Tell me what would make that work."
4. Bracket. Use when their ask sits far from your target: counter below your walk-away math, expecting to meet. Line: "That is a gap. If I go to X and you come to Y, we can close this today."
5. Walk-away line. Use once, calmly, when below floor: state it as fact, not threat. Line: "Below this number we cannot carry the scope, and I would rather say so now than discover it later."
6. Trade terms, not price. Use when price is the fight: payment upfront, multi-year, start date, reference, scope. Line: "The price holds if we can agree the term and the start date."
7. Re-anchor on value. Use when they fixate on the number: return to the cost of the problem first. Line: "Before we discuss the number, what is the problem worth solving for you?"
8. Conditional future. Use when they want a discount now: make it conditional on their later commitment. Line: "If we add the second team at renewal, I can revisit price then."
9. Budget question early. Use at the first pricing hint: ask what they were approved for. Line: "What number were you working with internally?"
10. Time-box the offer. Use only with a real deadline: attach your price to a date you can defend. Line: "This pricing holds until your number's date."

## Rules
- Never concede without naming what you get in return.
- Never invent a deadline, a floor or an approval limit you do not have.
- The walk-away line is used once per deal; repeating it makes it noise.
- No threats, no countdown pressure, no fake competing offers.
- Trade anything except the one thing you listed as non-tradeable.
- If the rep gave no walk-away point, ask for it before recommending any move.
