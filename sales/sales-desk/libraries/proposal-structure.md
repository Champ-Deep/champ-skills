# Proposal Structure

The section order of a winning proposal and what each section must prove, for drafting one or fixing a draft before it goes out.

## Give Claude
- What you are proposing: product, scope, price basis, in a few lines
- What you know of their situation: pain, decision process, timeline, pasted from your notes
- Optional: their requirements or RFP text, pasted
- Optional: a current draft to restructure, pasted

## Claude does
1. Output the section order below with what each section must prove and what goes in it.
2. Fill each section from your pasted notes; sections with no supporting input get marked "needs input: X".
3. If a draft was pasted, map its content into the order and list what is missing or sitting in the wrong place.
4. Cut anything in the draft that is a claim without proof, and list it separately so you substantiate or delete it.
5. Add the open questions the proposal must answer before it can be accepted, one line each.
6. State the acceptance path from your input only: who signs, what they agree to, by when.

## Output
Section order, each with what it must prove:
1. Executive summary: proves you understood the problem. One paragraph, their words, no product features.
2. Their situation and cost of inaction: proves the problem is worth money now. Facts from discovery only.
3. Proposed solution and scope: proves exactly what is and is not included. In-scope and out-of-scope lists.
4. How it works and rollout: proves the change is low risk. Steps, owners, dates from input.
5. Proof: proves others like them got the result. Only references and numbers you actually hold.
6. Pricing and terms: proves value against cost. One price, what it includes, what adds cost later.
7. Timeline and milestones: proves momentum. Dates, checkpoints, who does what.
8. Risks and mitigations: proves you thought about what can go wrong. Top three risks with answers.
9. Acceptance and next step: proves how this becomes a yes. Signature line, date, first action.
Plus: missing-input list (section, fact needed, where to get it) and cut list (claim, why unsupported).

## Rules
- Never invent numbers, dates, pricing, case studies or customer names to fill a section. Mark needs input.
- Keep the order as listed; if your process requires otherwise, state the change.
- Every claim needs proof you actually hold, otherwise it goes to the cut list.
- Price appears once, in its own section, with inclusions spelled out.
- If the input is too thin for a proposal, say so and list what to gather first.
- No adjectives of quality (powerful, leading, world-class) anywhere in the draft.
