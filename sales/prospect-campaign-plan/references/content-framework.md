# Content Framework Reference

Patterns for writing the strategy, email, and LinkedIn sections of the campaign plan. These are templates — fill in with real research findings, not generic placeholders.

---

## CHAMP Section Patterns

### Customer Persona Card

```
Persona A: [Role Title] at [Org Type]
Pain points: [3 specific pains derived from research]
Goals: [2 goals that map to the firm's offering]
Tone: [1 sentence: how they prefer to be spoken to]
Objections: [2 realistic objections to using this service]
Where they are: [channels they're active on]
```

### Hypothesis Statement Format

"We believe [primary audience segment] will engage with [company name] when they see [specific positioning angle], because [market insight or behavioral truth about this segment]."

Good example: "We believe UAE SME general counsels will engage with Sultan Al Hosani Legal when they see proactive legal intelligence (not just reactive advice), because in the DIFC/ADGM environment, legal surprises are career risk — prevention is more valuable than cure."

Avoid: Generic statements. Every hypothesis should be falsifiable and specific to this company.

| Signal from Research | Recommended Channels |

| Premium/luxury consumer | Instagram + Email + Influencer |
| Professional services (legal, finance, consulting) | LinkedIn + Email + Referral |

## Email Sequence Structure

### Email 1: Welcome / Authority

**Purpose**: Establish credibility and set expectations. This is not a sales email.

Structure:
1. Preheader: "What we believe about [industry topic]"
2. Hero: Company name + "Welcome" headline + one-sentence belief statement
3. Body: Thank you for subscribing + who we are + why we exist (2-3 short paragraphs)
4. Featured insight: One genuinely useful tip or framework (numbered list, 3 items)
5. Social proof: One testimonial (anonymized if needed)
6. What's coming: 2-3 bullets of what subscribers will receive
7. CTA: Visit website (low commitment)
8. Footer: Address, unsubscribe

Subject line formula: "Welcome to [Company] — [belief or promise about the industry]"
Alternative: "The [Company] approach to [core topic]: a quick intro"

### Email 2: Nurture / Insight (Persona-segmented)

**Purpose**: Demonstrate expertise. Build trust through value. No selling.

Structure:
1. Preheader: Hint at the key insight
2. Hero: "[Number] patterns/mistakes/insights we see in [industry]"
3. Numbered cards: 3 specific, researched insights relevant to this persona (not generic)
4. Testimonial: Real quote from a relevant client type
5. Soft CTA: "Would any of these apply to your [business/situation]?"
6. CTA button: Book a call / Get an audit / Learn more (low friction)

Subject line formula: "[Number] [things/patterns/mistakes] we see in [specific context]"
Alternative: "The [Company] guide to [specific relevant topic]"

Segmentation note: Write this email for the B2B persona if you have two personas. The B2C version shifts to personal outcomes and uses warmer language.

### Email 3: Conversion / CTA

**Purpose**: Drive a booking or inquiry. Time-scarcity framing (if authentic).

Structure:
1. Preheader: "A few slots left this week"
2. Hero: "[First Name], one more thing" or "[First Name], we've been thinking about you"
3. Bridge paragraph: Reference the value delivered in prior emails
4. The ask: Specific, low-commitment CTA (30-min call, free audit, no-obligation consultation)
5. What they get: 3-4 bullet points of specific value from the consultation
6. Objection handling: Address the 2 most common objections inline
7. CTA button: Primary action (bold, high contrast)
8. No-obligation line: "No commitment. No jargon. Just clarity."
9. P.S. line: Alternative CTA (reply to this email)

Subject line formula: "[First Name], [number] slots left for [specific thing]"
Alternative: "[First Name], ready when you are"

## LinkedIn Post Templates

### Format 1: Thought Leadership Hook (Listicle)

**Structure**:
```
[Bold hook line — a counterintuitive claim or surprising number]

[2-sentence context that earns the reader's attention]

Here are [4-5 specific things] [relevant audience] needs to know:

1. [Specific item] — [1-2 sentence explanation with real detail]
2. [Specific item] — [1-2 sentence explanation]
3. [Specific item] — [1-2 sentence explanation]
4. [Specific item] — [1-2 sentence explanation]

[1-sentence synthesis or takeaway]

[Soft CTA: question to drive comments OR link to resource]
```

Hook formula examples:
- "Most [audience] don't know this [topic] changed in [year]."
- "[Number]% of [situation] we review have this problem."
- "I've seen [N] [things] in [timeframe]. Here's the pattern."

### Format 2: Regulatory/News Alert

**Structure**:
```
🚨 [Specific regulation/change] — [Date or Q]:

[1-sentence plain-English summary of what changed]

What this means for [specific audience]:
- [Impact 1]
- [Impact 2]
- [Impact 3]

[What to do next — specific action, not vague advice]

[CTA: DM for guidance / link to resource / comment below]
```

Timing note: This format only works when there is a real, recent, specific development. Do not fabricate. If no regulatory change is recent, use the other formats instead.

### Format 3: Anonymized Case Insight

**Structure**:
```
A [client type] came to us with [specific problem].

Here's what we found, and what we did about it:

[2-3 sentences: the situation, with specific details that feel real]

What we found: [Specific insight — the unexpected angle]

What we did: [Specific action]

Result: [Concrete outcome with metrics if possible]

[1-sentence lesson that generalizes to the audience]

[Soft CTA: "Facing something similar? DM us." or link]
```

Key: The case must feel specific and real (even if anonymized). Generic cases ("a client had a problem, we solved it") get no engagement. Specific cases ("a DIFC-registered fintech found a cross-border data compliance issue in their employment contracts") get shared.

## Content Calendar Logic

### 12-Week Structure (Monthly Themes)

**Month 1 (Weeks 1-4): Awareness + Authority**
- Theme: Establish presence. Show expertise. No selling.
- Weekly output: 1 LinkedIn post + 1 email (Week 1) + 1 blog or resource (optional)
- Content types: Thought leadership, "who we are", industry explainers

**Month 2 (Weeks 5-8): Engagement + Trust**
- Theme: Demonstrate depth. Start segmenting. Show social proof.
- Weekly output: 2 LinkedIn posts + 1 nurture email (Week 6)
- Content types: Case insights, regulatory alerts, testimonials, data-backed claims

**Month 3 (Weeks 9-12): Conversion + Referral**
- Theme: Drive consultations. Activate warm leads. Encourage referrals.
- Weekly output: 2 LinkedIn posts + 1 conversion email (Week 10) + retargeting ads (if budget)
- Content types: CTA-heavy content, limited spots messaging, referral prompts

## KPI Framework

### Metrics by Funnel Stage

**Awareness (top)**
- LinkedIn Post Impressions (weekly)
- LinkedIn Follower Growth (monthly)
- Website Sessions from Social (weekly)
- Blog/Content Page Views (monthly)

**Engagement (middle)**
- Email Open Rate (per send)
- Email Click-Through Rate (per send)
- LinkedIn Engagement Rate (per post)
- Time on Page (monthly)

**Conversion (bottom)**
- Consultation Booking Rate
- Lead Form Submissions
- Cost Per Lead (if paid)
- Qualified Lead to Consultation Conversion %

**North star**: Consultation bookings. Every other metric feeds this.

### Baseline Notes

For Day 1 baselines, use:
- "Measure on Day 1" for metrics requiring a live account
- "Current (via GA4)" for website metrics
- Industry benchmarks as a proxy: Email open rate industry avg 25-30%, LinkedIn engagement rate 2-5%
